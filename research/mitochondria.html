Mitochondrial dynamics

Mitochondria constantly divide and fuse. Watching them live at super-resolution shows where they divide and what physical forces decide whether a constriction finishes.

Membrane tension decides whether a constriction divides

During fission, molecular machinery gathers on the outer mitochondrial membrane and constricts it. Constrictions can either go on to divide or relax again, and whether membrane tension plays a role had long been suspected but never shown. Using live-cell structured illumination microscopy, we measured the diameters of thin tubules pulled from mitochondria. We also used a fluorescence-lifetime membrane tension sensor targeted to mitochondria.

Both showed that mitochondria are under tension. When we lowered that tension, constrictions started just as often but were less likely to complete. A physical model based on our estimates of membrane tension and bending energy in living cells explains the measured probability that a constriction divides.

Tools to see membrane tension

This work relied on mechanosensitive "flipper" probes developed with chemistry and biochemistry colleagues in Geneva. The probes report membrane tension through changes in fluorescence lifetime, and versions targeted to mitochondria, the ER and lysosomes are now available to the community.
