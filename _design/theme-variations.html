About

I'm a physicist with a passion for experimental and quantitative biology. I build and apply new microscopy strategies to uncover the physical rules that govern biological processes.

As a Research Associate in the lab of Prof. Johan Paulsson at Harvard Medical School, I combine advanced microscopy with microfluidics to study how plasmids control their own replication.

For my PhD with Prof. Suliana Manley at EPFL, I developed high-throughput and adaptive super-resolution methods, and used them to look at organelles in new ways, from the architecture of centrioles to randomness in mitochondrial division.

Research

Current

Plasmid replication control

Single-cell imaging and microfluidics to investigate how plasmids control their own replication.

Methods

High-throughput super-resolution

Homogeneous multifocal illumination that turns super-resolution into a large field-of-view technique.

Methods

Event-driven microscopy

Smart microscopes that detect rare events in real time and adapt acquisition on the fly.

Biology

Mitochondrial dynamics

Live-cell super-resolution imaging of where and why mitochondria divide, and the role of chance in fission.
